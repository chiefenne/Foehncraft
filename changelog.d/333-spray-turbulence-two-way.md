- **ECN Spray G on k-ω SST.** The late lead of the G1 case came from the turbulence model, not the spray: the plumes are gas jets driven by the drops, and the k-ε family keeps their turbulence low. On SST the liquid is 15 % and the vapour 7 % ahead of the measurement at 1.1 ms, against 30 % and 22 % on k-ε and 34 % and 26 % on ζ-f0; the mesh, the breakup, the gas density and the injected momentum were ruled out. The case now runs on SST; `tools/plot/spray_g_g1.py` names its comparison run. Gallery 323, recipe 323.
- **The drops damp the gas turbulence (D330).** `[spray.coupling] turbulence = "dispersion-work"` takes the work the eddies of the dispersion do on the drops out of k, with the ε (or ω) term of KIVA-II (c_s = 1.5). Only the part of the drop's motion that follows its eddy is booked, so the term is never positive and free of the noise of the mean slip, which broke the first runs. `spray.csv` gets `gas_turbulence_w`, `result.json` `gas_turbulence_work`. Checks: drops born in turbulent gas take 0.88 of −2k times their mass from their first eddies; in a lumped cell k loses the booked work over ρV within 3 %. On ECN Spray G it removes nearly all of the plume turbulence and the liquid runs 33 % ahead at 1.1 ms instead of 15 %, so it is off by default and in the case. Gallery 333, recipe 333.
- **At most 32 eddies per parcel and step.** Where k is nearly zero the eddies of the dispersion become so short that every eddy ended a sub-step and the parcels filled the memory. Beyond `MAX_EDDIES_PER_STEP` the eddy now lasts to the end of the step, counted as `held_eddies` in `result.json`; runs in developed turbulence never reach it.
